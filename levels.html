CEFR levels explained

The Common European Framework of Reference (CEFR) describes language ability on six levels, from A1 to C2. This library covers the four upper levels.

LevelWhat you can do
B1 IntermediateHandle everyday situations, describe experiences and give simple reasons for opinions.
B2 Upper intermediateTalk fluently with native speakers on a wide range of topics and argue a point of view.
C1 AdvancedUse English flexibly for social, academic and professional purposes, with well-structured, detailed text.
C2 ProficiencyUnderstand virtually everything and express yourself precisely, with fine shades of meaning.

Finding your level

  • If you can follow a TV series with subtitles but miss jokes and fast dialogue, start at B1 or B2.
  • If you read news articles comfortably but your writing still sounds simple, work on C1.
  • If people rarely notice you are not a native speaker, refine your style with C2.
Tip. Study one level below your reading level for grammar. Gaps at lower levels are the most common cause of persistent mistakes.

How each level grows

Each step takes roughly 150 to 250 hours of guided study. Mix grammar lessons with plenty of reading and listening at your level, and review vocabulary with the quiz mode on each vocabulary page.
