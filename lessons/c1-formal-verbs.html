Formal verbs for phrasal verbs

Swap everyday phrasal verbs for one-word formal verbs in essays, reports and professional emails.

Download PDF

Phrasal verbs are natural in conversation but can sound too informal in academic and business writing. Most have a one-word equivalent, often from Latin, that fits a formal register.

  • investigateverb

    = look into

    The committee will investigate the complaint.

  • postponeverb

    = put off

    The launch has been postponed until May.

  • tolerateverb

    = put up with

    The company will not tolerate late payments.

  • discoververb

    = find out

    Researchers discovered a link between sleep and memory.

  • establishverb

    = set up

    The charity was established in 2010.

  • reduceverb

    = cut down on

    We need to reduce energy use.

  • eliminateverb

    = get rid of

    The new system eliminates manual errors.

  • examineverb

    = go over

    The report examines three possible solutions.

  • withdrawverb

    = pull out (of)

    Two partners withdrew from the project.

  • compensate forverb

    = make up for

    Higher salaries cannot fully compensate for long hours.

  • consultverb

    = talk to (an expert)

    Consult your manager before signing.

  • increaseverb

    = go up

    Prices increased by 4% last year.

Keep the register consistent. One formal verb in a chatty paragraph sounds odd, and so does one phrasal verb in a formal report. Decide the register before you write.
Common mistake. Keeping the particle: investigate into and discuss about are wrong. The formal verb usually takes a direct object: investigate the problem, discuss the plan.

Practice

Replace the phrasal verb with a formal verb.

  1. We have to put off the meeting until next week.
  2. The police are looking into the accident.
  3. The government wants to cut down on plastic waste.
  4. Sales went up sharply in the second quarter.
  5. The firm was set up by two engineers.
  6. Correct the mistake: The report discusses about the main risks.
