Future perfect and future continuous

Look back from a point in the future and describe actions in progress then, with precise future forms.

Download PDF

At C1 you need more than will and going to. The future continuous and the two future perfect forms let you place an action exactly in relation to a future moment.

FormUseExample
will be + -ingAn action in progress at a future timeThis time tomorrow I'll be flying to London.
will be + -ingA polite question about plansWill you be using the car tonight?
will have + past participleCompleted before a future timeBy June, we'll have finished the project.
will have been + -ingDuration up to a future timeIn May, I'll have been working here for ten years.
be due to / be about toScheduled or very near futureThe train is due to arrive at six. The film is about to start.
Key time words. By (the time), by then, by the end of usually signal the future perfect. This time next week, at 8 tomorrow usually signal the future continuous.
Common mistake. After by the time, when, before use the present, not will: By the time you arrive, I'll have left, not By the time you will arrive.

Practice

  1. Complete: By 2030, scientists ___ (find) a cure.
  2. Complete: Don't call at nine; I ___ (have) dinner.
  3. Complete: Next month she ___ (teach) for twenty years.
  4. Correct the mistake: By the time he will get here, the meeting will have ended.
  5. Ask politely about someone's plans: use the printer this afternoon.
Show answers
  1. will have found
  2. will be having
  3. will have been teaching
  4. By the time he gets here, the meeting will have ended.
  5. Will you be using the printer this afternoon?
