Making polite requests

Ask for things politely in shops, at work and with friends, and know which form fits each situation.

Download PDF

In English, how you ask matters as much as what you ask. A direct command can sound rude even when you do not mean it. Longer, softer questions sound more polite.

FormHow politeExample
Can you...?Friendly, for people you knowCan you pass the salt?
Could you...?Polite, for most situationsCould you send me the file?
Would you mind + -ing?Very politeWould you mind closing the window?
Could I / May I...?Asking for permissionCould I borrow your pen? May I come in?
I'd like... / Could I have...?Ordering in a shop or caféI'd like a coffee, please. Could I have the bill?
Answering Would you mind...? No, not at all means "yes, I'll do it", because you are saying you do not mind.
Common mistake. Translating a direct Arabic request word for word: Give me a coffee sounds rude in English. Say Could I have a coffee, please? And remember please and thank you even with friends.

Practice

Make each request more polite.

  1. (to a waiter) Bring me the menu.
  2. (to your manager) Check my report.
  3. (to a stranger on a train) Move your bag.
  4. (to a teacher) I want to leave early.
  5. (to a friend) Lend me 10 dollars.
  6. Someone asks "Would you mind waiting a moment?" You are happy to wait. What do you say?
Show answers
  1. Could I have the menu, please?
  2. Could you check my report, please? / Would you mind checking my report?
  3. Would you mind moving your bag, please?
  4. Could I leave early today, please? / May I leave early?
  5. Can you lend me 10 dollars?
  6. No, not at all. / No problem.
