Agreeing and disagreeing politely

Share your view in discussions and exams: agree strongly, agree in part, or disagree without sounding rude.

Download PDF

In discussions, speaking exams and meetings you need to react to other people's ideas. Strong disagreement is fine in English if you soften it and give a reason.

FunctionPhrases
Strong agreementAbsolutely. I couldn't agree more. That's exactly what I think.
Partial agreementI see what you mean, but... That's true to some extent, however...
Polite disagreementI'm not sure I agree. I see it a bit differently. I take your point, but...
Asking for an opinionWhat do you think? How do you feel about...? Would you agree that...?
Giving a reasonThe main reason is... For example,... That's why I think...
Agree before you disagree. Starting with something you accept (That's a fair point, but...) makes the other person more ready to listen.
Common mistakes. I am agree is wrong: say I agree. And avoid a flat You are wrong; say I'm not sure that's right or I see it differently.

Practice

  1. Correct the mistake: I am agree with you.
  2. Make polite: You're wrong about the price.
  3. Agree strongly with: "We should learn English every day."
  4. Agree in part with: "Social media is bad for teenagers."
  5. Ask a classmate for their opinion about online learning.
Show answers
  1. I agree with you.
  2. I'm not sure that's the right price. / I think the price might be different.
  3. Absolutely, I couldn't agree more.
  4. That's true to some extent, but it can also help them stay in touch with friends. (any partial agreement + reason)
  5. What do you think about online learning? / How do you feel about online learning?
