Connected speech: linking, weak forms and elision

Understand natural, fast English by recognising how sounds join, shrink and disappear between words.

Download PDF

In natural speech, words are not separated by pauses. Sounds link together, small grammar words lose their vowels, and some sounds vanish. Knowing these patterns helps you hear words you already know but fail to recognise at speed.

FeatureWhat happensWritten / How it sounds
LinkingA final consonant joins the next vowelturn it off → tur-ni-toff
Weak formsGrammar words lose their stressa cup of tea → a cup-uh tea; for you → fuh you
ElisionA /t/ or /d/ disappears between consonantsnext week → nex week; handbag → hanbag
AssimilationA sound changes to suit the next oneten pounds → tem pounds; did you → didja
ContractionsAuxiliaries shortenI would have → I'd've; you are not → you're not / you aren't

Listen

The recording is read by your device's built-in English voice. Listen first, then open the transcript to check.

Show transcript

Did you get a chance to look at the proposal? I'd've sent it sooner, but I couldn't get hold of the figures till last night. Most of it's fine, I think. The only thing I'm not sure about is the budget for next quarter. We've asked for a lot more than last time, and they're bound to question it. If you've got a minute this afternoon, could we go through it together? Just let me know what time suits you, and I'll book a room.

Listen for stressed words. Content words such as nouns, main verbs and adjectives keep their stress. Catch those first, then let grammar fill the gaps: GET a CHANCE to LOOK at the proPOsal.
Common mistake. Trying to hear every word clearly. Native speakers don't pronounce every word fully, so listen for meaning in chunks, not single words.

Practice

Listen at normal speed first, then use the transcript.

  1. How is "Did you" likely to sound at the start?
  2. What is the full form of "I'd've"?
  3. In "last night", which sound is likely to disappear?
  4. In "Most of it's fine", how do "of" and "it's" link?
  5. Why is the speaker worried about the budget?
Show answers
  1. "Didja" (assimilation of /d/ + /j/)
  2. I would have
  3. The /t/ at the end of "last": "las night"
  4. The /v/ of "of" joins the vowel of "it's": "o-vit's".
  5. They asked for much more than last time, so it will probably be questioned.
