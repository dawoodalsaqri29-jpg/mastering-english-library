Adjectives for feelings

Twelve adjectives to describe how you feel, plus the -ed and -ing difference that catches many learners.

Download PDF

Using exact feeling words makes your English sound natural and helps people understand you. Many of these adjectives have two forms: -ed for how a person feels and -ing for the thing that causes the feeling.

  • bored / boringadjective

    feeling that nothing is interesting / not interesting

    I was bored because the film was boring.

  • excited / excitingadjective

    very happy and full of energy about something

    The children are excited about the trip.

  • worriedadjective

    thinking that something bad may happen

    She's worried about her exam.

  • upsetadjective

    unhappy because something bad happened

    He was upset when he lost his phone.

  • embarrassedadjective

    feeling silly or ashamed in front of others

    I was embarrassed when I forgot her name.

  • relievedadjective

    happy because a worry has gone

    We were relieved to hear the flight was safe.

  • disappointedadjective

    sad because something was not as good as you hoped

    I was disappointed with my score.

  • confusedadjective

    not able to understand

    The instructions made me confused.

  • proudadjective

    pleased about something you or someone close did

    My parents are proud of me.

  • nervousadjective

    a little afraid before something important

    I always feel nervous before interviews.

  • gratefuladjective

    wanting to thank someone

    I'm grateful for your help.

  • homesickadjective

    sad because you are away from home

    She felt homesick during her first month abroad.

Common mistake. I am boring means you make other people bored. To describe your feeling, say I am bored.

Practice

  1. The lesson was so ___ that I nearly fell asleep. (bored / boring)
  2. I passed my driving test! I'm so ___ .
  3. She forgot her lines on stage and felt very ___ .
  4. We were ___ when the doctor said it was nothing serious.
  5. He's ___ about the job interview tomorrow.
  6. I'm really ___ for everything you've done for me.
Show answers
  1. boring
  2. excited / proud
  3. embarrassed
  4. relieved
  5. nervous / worried
  6. grateful
