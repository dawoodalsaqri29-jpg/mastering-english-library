Fact or opinion?

Separate what can be checked from what the writer believes, a key skill for news, reviews and exam texts.

Download PDF

A fact can be checked and shown to be true or false. An opinion is what someone thinks or feels. Most texts mix the two, and good readers notice where one ends and the other begins.

Signals of opinionExamples
Opinion phrasesI believe, in my view, it seems that, clearly
Judgement adjectivesbest, worst, amazing, disappointing, unfair
Modal verbs of advice or predictionshould, ought to, will probably
GeneralisationsEveryone knows... Nobody wants...

Text

The new metro line opened in March and carries about 40,000 passengers a day. Tickets cost 2 riyals, half the price of a taxi ride across the city. It is the best thing to happen to public transport in years. Clearly, the government should build a second line as soon as possible, because nobody wants to sit in traffic any more.

Numbers are not always facts. About 40,000 passengers can be checked; the best thing in years cannot, even though it sounds certain.
Common mistake. Treating confident language as fact. Words like clearly and obviously often introduce an opinion the writer wants you to accept without proof.

Practice

Write F (fact) or O (opinion).

  1. The new metro line opened in March.
  2. It carries about 40,000 passengers a day.
  3. Tickets cost 2 riyals.
  4. It is the best thing to happen to public transport in years.
  5. The government should build a second line.
  6. Nobody wants to sit in traffic any more.
Show answers
  1. F
  2. F
  3. F
  4. O
  5. O
  6. O (a generalisation that cannot be proved)
