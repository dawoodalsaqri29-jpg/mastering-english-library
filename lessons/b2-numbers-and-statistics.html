Catching numbers and statistics

Note percentages, large numbers, dates and trends accurately in a short news report.

Download PDF

Reports and lectures are full of numbers, and they go past quickly. Prepare for the forms numbers take, and listen for the words that tell you whether a figure rose or fell.

TypeHow it soundsWritten
Percentagestwelve point five per cent12.5%
Large numbersthree point two million3,200,000
Fractionsnearly a third, more than halfabout 33%, over 50%
Yearstwenty twenty-four, two thousand and nine2024, 2009
Trendsrose, climbed, fell, dropped, remained steadyup, down, no change

Listen

The recording is read by your device's built-in English voice. Listen first, then open the transcript to check.

Show transcript

Tourism figures for last year are now in. The country welcomed three point two million visitors, up from two point eight million the year before. Hotel occupancy rose to seventy-four per cent, while the average stay fell slightly, from five nights to four point six. Visitors from Europe made up more than half of the total, and nearly a third came from other Gulf countries. Officials expect numbers to climb again this year, aiming for four million by twenty twenty-seven.

Write the number first, then the trend. Jot 3.2m ↑ (2.8m) rather than a sentence. Check the unit: per cent, million, nights.
Common mistake. Confusing thirteen and thirty, or fifteen and fifty. The -teen numbers are stressed at the end; the -ty numbers at the start.

Practice

  1. How many visitors came last year?
  2. How many came the year before?
  3. What was the hotel occupancy rate?
  4. Did the average stay go up or down? From what to what?
  5. What fraction of visitors came from other Gulf countries?
  6. What is the target, and for which year?
Show answers
  1. 3.2 million
  2. 2.8 million
  3. 74%
  4. Down, from 5 nights to 4.6 nights
  5. Nearly a third
  6. 4 million visitors by 2027
