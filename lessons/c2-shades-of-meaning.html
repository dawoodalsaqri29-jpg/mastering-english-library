Shades of meaning: from mild to strong

Choose the exact word on a scale of intensity, so your English says precisely what you mean.

Download PDF

At C2, accuracy means choosing the word with the right strength. Annoyed, irritated and livid all describe anger, but they paint very different pictures. Learn synonyms as ladders, from mildest to strongest.

  • annoyed → exasperated → lividadjective

    angry, from mild to extreme

    She was livid when the flight was cancelled for the third time.

  • concerned → anxious → distraughtadjective

    worried, from mild to overwhelming

    The family was distraught after the accident.

  • pleased → delighted → elatedadjective

    happy, from mild to intense

    He was elated to win the scholarship.

  • tired → exhausted → drainedadjective

    lacking energy, ending in complete emptiness

    After the night shift she felt completely drained.

  • dislike → detest → loatheverb

    not like, from mild to intense hatred

    I loathe waiting in queues.

  • glance → look → stare → gazeverb

    ways of looking: quick, neutral, fixed and rude, long and thoughtful

    She gazed at the sea for hours.

  • chuckle → laugh → roarverb

    laughing, from quiet to very loud

    The audience roared at the final joke.

  • slim → thin → skinny → emaciatedadjective

    not fat, from positive to worrying

    "Slim" is a compliment; "skinny" usually is not.

Watch the connotation too. Words at the same strength can be positive or negative: slim praises, skinny criticises; thrifty praises, stingy criticises.
Common mistake. Using intensifiers with already strong words: very livid or very delighted sound wrong. Use absolutely or utterly: absolutely livid, utterly exhausted.

Practice

  1. Order from mildest to strongest: elated, pleased, delighted.
  2. Choose: After the marathon she was absolutely (tired / exhausted).
  3. Choose the positive word: (slim / skinny).
  4. Replace very angry with one stronger word: The manager was very angry about the data leak.
  5. Which verb suggests looking for a long time, thoughtfully? (glance / stare / gaze)
Show answers
  1. pleased, delighted, elated
  2. exhausted
  3. slim
  4. livid / furious (The manager was livid about the data leak.)
  5. gaze
