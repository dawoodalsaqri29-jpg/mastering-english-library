The subjunctive in formal English

Use the base-form subjunctive after verbs like insist and recommend, and were for unreal situations.

Download PDF

The subjunctive is rare in everyday English but common in formal writing, law, and set phrases. It is easy to recognise once you know the two main patterns.

PatternFormExample
Mandative subjunctiveverb/adjective + that + subject + base form (no -s, no tense)The board insisted that he resign. It is essential that she be informed.
Common trigger verbsinsist, recommend, suggest, demand, request, propose, requireWe recommend that the meeting be postponed.
Common trigger adjectivesessential, vital, crucial, imperative, importantIt is vital that every student attend.
Were subjunctivewere for all subjects in unreal situationsIf I were you, I'd accept. She acts as if she were the manager.
Fixed expressionsformulaic phrasesLong live the king. So be it. Be that as it may,...
The negative has no do. Put not before the base form: The doctor recommended that he not drive for a week.
Common mistake. Adding -s or a past tense after the trigger: They demanded that he pays or ...that he paid. In formal English say that he pay. British English also accepts that he should pay.

Practice

  1. The committee recommended that the project ___ (delay).
  2. It is essential that every employee ___ (complete) the training.
  3. Correct the mistake: She insisted that he leaves immediately.
  4. Make negative: The lawyer advised that the client ___ (sign) anything yet.
  5. Complete with the subjunctive: If he ___ more careful, he wouldn't make these mistakes.
  6. Rewrite with should (British style): We suggest that she apply early.
Show answers
  1. be delayed
  2. complete
  3. She insisted that he leave immediately.
  4. not sign
  5. were
  6. We suggest that she should apply early.
