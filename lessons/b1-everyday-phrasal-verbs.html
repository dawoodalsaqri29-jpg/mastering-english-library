Everyday phrasal verbs

Twelve common phrasal verbs for daily life, with examples you can reuse.

Phrasal verbs are verbs plus a small word such as up, out or on. The meaning often changes completely, so learn them with an example.

  • get upverb

    to leave your bed after sleeping

    I usually get up at seven.

  • look forverb

    to try to find something

    I'm looking for my phone.

  • find outverb

    to discover information

    Can you find out when the train leaves?

  • give upverb

    to stop trying or stop a habit

    He gave up smoking last year.

  • turn on / turn offverb

    to start or stop a machine or light

    Please turn off the lights.

  • pick upverb

    to collect someone or something

    I'll pick you up at the station.

  • run out ofverb

    to have none left

    We've run out of milk.

  • look afterverb

    to take care of

    She looks after her younger brother.

  • put onverb

    to start wearing clothes

    Put on your coat, it's cold.

  • set offverb

    to start a journey

    We set off early to avoid traffic.

  • come backverb

    to return

    When are you coming back?

  • fill inverb

    to complete a form

    Please fill in this form.

  2. Can you ___ my cat while I'm on holiday?
  3. Don't ___! You're nearly there.
