Irony and understatement

Read between the lines: when writers say less, or the opposite, of what they mean.

Download PDF

At C2, the hardest reading questions are about tone. Writers in journalism, reviews and fiction often mean more than, or the opposite of, what their words literally say.

DeviceExampleReal meaning
UnderstatementThe hotel was not exactly spotless.It was dirty.
Litotes (a negative to express a positive)Her performance was not bad at all.It was very good.
Verbal ironyAnother two-hour delay. Brilliant.This is terrible.
Overstatement for ironyThe minister's three-word answer was a masterpiece of detail.The answer said almost nothing.
Scare quotesThe "renovated" kitchen had no oven.The writer does not accept the word.
Check for a mismatch. Irony usually shows a clash between the words and the facts around them. If praise sits next to bad news, read it again.
Common mistake. Taking polite British understatement literally. It's a bit of a problem can mean the situation is serious.

Text

The launch of the new app went about as smoothly as these things usually do. Within an hour, the "intuitive" login page had locked out half of its users, and the help line, staffed by a single cheerful robot, was a model of efficiency, answering every question with the same apology. Still, the company's statement that the day had been "a learning experience" was, if nothing else, accurate.

Practice

  1. What does about as smoothly as these things usually do suggest?
  2. Why is intuitive in quotation marks?
  3. What is ironic about a model of efficiency?
  4. What does the writer mean by if nothing else, accurate?
  5. Overall tone: (a) admiring (b) sarcastic (c) neutral
Show answers
  1. That it did not go smoothly at all; launches often go wrong.
  2. They are scare quotes: the writer thinks the page was not intuitive.
  3. Giving everyone the same apology is useless, the opposite of efficient.
  4. The only true thing about the statement is that the company had a lot to learn, because so much went wrong.
  5. (b) sarcastic
