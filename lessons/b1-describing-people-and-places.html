Describing people and places

Describe someone you know or a place you love, with the right order of details and plenty of useful adjectives.

Download PDF

Describing people and places comes up in conversations and in almost every speaking exam. A good description moves from general to specific and adds a personal opinion at the end.

StepPeoplePlaces
GeneralMy uncle is in his fifties and lives in Al Ain.It's a small fishing village on the east coast.
AppearanceHe's tall, with grey hair and a big smile.There are white houses and a long sandy beach.
Character / atmosphereHe's generous and very funny.It's quiet and peaceful, especially in the morning.
ExampleHe always tells jokes at family dinners.You can watch the boats come in at sunset.
Your feelingI really admire him.It's my favourite place to relax.
Give an example for character words. She's kind is fine; She's kind: she always helps her neighbours with their shopping sounds much better.
Common mistakes. What does he look like? asks about appearance; What is he like? asks about character. And say He has long hair, not He has a long hair.

Practice

  1. Begin a description of your best friend with one general sentence.
  2. Correct the mistake: My sister has a long black hair.
  3. Answer: "What's your brother like?" (character, not appearance)
  4. Describe the atmosphere of a busy market in one sentence.
  5. Add an example to: My teacher is very patient.
Show answers
  1. For example: My best friend, Khalid, is twenty-five and works as an engineer.
  2. My sister has long black hair.
  3. For example: He's friendly and hard-working, but a bit shy at first.
  4. For example: It's noisy and colourful, and it smells of spices and fresh bread.
  5. My teacher is very patient: she explains things again and again until everyone understands.
