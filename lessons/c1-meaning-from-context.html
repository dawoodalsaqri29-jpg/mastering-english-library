Working out meaning from context

Guess unknown words from the clues around them instead of reaching for a dictionary.

Download PDF

Advanced texts always contain some unfamiliar words. Strong readers do not stop at each one; they use the context to work out a meaning that is good enough to keep reading.

ClueExampleWhat it tells you
Definition or rephrasingThe drug is efficacious, that is, it works.The meaning is given directly.
ContrastUnlike her gregarious sister, Maya prefers to be alone.The opposite of preferring to be alone: sociable.
ExampleArid regions, such as deserts, get little rain.Very dry.
Cause and resultThe talks foundered, so no agreement was signed.Failed.
Word partsun-precedent-edNever happened before.
Three-step check. Decide the word class (noun, verb, adjective), decide if it is positive or negative, then try a simple word in its place and see if the sentence still makes sense.
Common mistake. Guessing from how a word looks alone. Inflammable looks like "not flammable", but it means "easily set on fire". Always check your guess against the sentence.

Practice

Work out the meaning of the word in bold and name the clue you used.

  1. The manager was adamant: no matter how much we argued, she would not change her decision.
  2. Most of his novels are long, but this one is remarkably terse.
  3. The town relies on perennial crops, plants such as fruit trees that grow back every year.
  4. Prices rose so precipitously that many families could no longer afford bread.
  5. Her misgivings about the plan proved right when the project collapsed.
Show answers
  1. Refusing to change one's mind (cause and result: "would not change her decision").
  2. Short, using few words (contrast with "long").
  3. Living or growing for many years (example and definition: "fruit trees that grow back every year").
  4. Very suddenly and steeply (result: families could no longer afford bread).
  5. Doubts or worries (result: the plan failed, so she was right to worry; word parts: mis- + giving).
