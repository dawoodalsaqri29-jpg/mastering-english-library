Describing and comparing photos

Talk for a minute about a picture: say what you see, speculate about it, and compare two photos.

Download PDF

Many speaking exams ask you to talk about photos for about a minute. Examiners want more than a list of objects: they want you to organise your description, guess what is happening, and compare.

StepUseful language
Give the general pictureThis photo shows a family having a meal outdoors.
Describe positionIn the foreground... In the background... On the left...
SpeculateThey might be celebrating something. It looks as if it's a holiday. She seems to be...
CompareWhereas the first photo is outdoors, the second is in an office. Both photos show...
Give your viewPersonally, I'd prefer... because...
Speculate with modals. You don't know the real story, so use might, could, must and it looks like / as if. They must be tired; they've clearly been walking all day.
Common mistakes. In the photo I can see a man, a table, a dog... is a list, not a description. And remember: It looks like a party (+ noun). Before a clause, It looks as if they're having a party is the safer choice in exams; It looks like they're having a party is common but informal.

Practice

  1. Start a description of a photo of students in a library.
  2. Speculate: A man is running to a bus stop with his bag open.
  3. Make it more formal: It looks like they are very happy.
  4. Compare: Photo A shows a busy city street; Photo B shows a quiet village.
  5. Give your view with a reason: Which place would you prefer to live in?
Show answers
  1. This photo shows a group of students studying together in a library.
  2. He might be late for work. / He must be in a hurry; it looks as if he's about to miss the bus.
  3. It looks as if they are very happy. (or: They look very happy.)
  4. Whereas Photo A shows a busy city street, Photo B shows a quiet village. Both are places where people live, but the pace of life seems very different.
  5. For example: Personally, I'd prefer the village because it's calmer and closer to nature.
