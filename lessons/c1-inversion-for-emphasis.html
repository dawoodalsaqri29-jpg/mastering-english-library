Inversion for emphasis

Using 'Never have I...', 'Not only...' and 'Little did they...' in formal writing.

After certain negative or restrictive expressions placed at the start of a sentence, English inverts the subject and auxiliary, as in a question. This adds emphasis and a formal tone.

Opening expressionExample
Never / Rarely / SeldomRarely have I seen such dedication.
Not only ... but alsoNot only did she win, but she also broke the record.
Hardly / Scarcely ... whenHardly had we arrived when it started to rain.
No sooner ... thanNo sooner had he sat down than the phone rang.
LittleLittle did they know what was coming.
Only then / Only after / Only whenOnly when I left did I realise my mistake.
Under no circumstancesUnder no circumstances should you open this door.
Need an auxiliary? If the normal sentence has no auxiliary, add do / does / did: She rarely complains becomes Rarely does she complain.
With 'only', the inversion is in the main clause. Only after the meeting did I understand, not Only after did the meeting I understand.

Practice

  1. I have never been so embarrassed. → Never ___
  2. He didn't know that he was being followed. → Little ___
  3. As soon as she got home, she fell asleep. → No sooner ___
  4. You must not tell anyone. → Under no circumstances ___
Show answers
  1. Never have I been so embarrassed.
  2. Little did he know that he was being followed.
  3. No sooner had she got home than she fell asleep.
  4. Under no circumstances must you tell anyone.
