Writing a formal complaint email

Complain clearly and politely, explain the problem with facts, and ask for a specific solution.

Download PDF

A good complaint is firm but polite. It states the facts, explains the effect on you, and asks for a clear action. Angry or vague complaints are easier to ignore.

ParagraphPurposeUseful language
OpeningSay why you are writingI am writing to complain about the laptop I ordered on 3 May (order no. 48213).
The problemFacts: what, when, what went wrongThe screen stopped working after two days, and the charger was missing.
The effectWhy it matters to youAs a result, I was unable to complete an important project.
The requestThe exact solution you want, and whenI would therefore like a full refund within 14 days.
ClosingFormal endingI look forward to your reply. Yours sincerely, ...
Be specific. Dates, order numbers and amounts make a complaint credible. I would like a replacement is stronger than Please do something.
Common mistakes. Informal phrases such as I'm really angry!! or Thanks, bye. Use I was very disappointed and Yours sincerely (when you know the name) or Yours faithfully (after Dear Sir or Madam).

Practice

Make each sentence suitable for a formal complaint.

  1. Hi guys, I'm writing because your delivery was rubbish.
  2. The food was cold and the waiter was super rude.
  3. Give me my money back now.
  4. Write a closing line for an email that started "Dear Sir or Madam".
  5. Add the effect: The hotel lost my booking. ___
Show answers
  1. Dear Sir or Madam, I am writing to complain about a late delivery.
  2. The food was served cold and the waiter was impolite.
  3. I would like a full refund, please. / I would be grateful if you could refund the full amount.
  4. I look forward to hearing from you. Yours faithfully, ...
  5. As a result, I had to find another hotel late at night and pay a higher price.
