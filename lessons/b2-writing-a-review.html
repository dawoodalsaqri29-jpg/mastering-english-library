Writing a review

Write a lively, balanced review of a film, book, restaurant or product that ends with a clear recommendation.

Download PDF

A review informs and entertains. Readers want to know what something is like, what is good and bad about it, and whether they should try it. B2 exams often ask for a review of about 150 to 200 words.

ParagraphPurposeUseful language
1. IntroductionName it and hook the readerIf you're looking for a quiet place to eat, Al Bustan is worth a visit.
2. DescriptionPlot, setting or main featuresSet in 1920s Cairo, the film follows a young journalist who...
3. Strengths and weaknessesGive your opinion with reasonsWhat impressed me most was... The only drawback was...
4. RecommendationSay who would enjoy itI'd highly recommend it to anyone who loves history. / Give it a miss if...
Use strong, precise adjectives. Replace good and bad with words like gripping, stunning, overpriced, predictable, cosy. Address the reader directly with questions: Have you ever wondered...?
Common mistake. Retelling the whole story. Keep the description short, avoid spoilers, and spend most of your words on your opinion and reasons.

Practice

  1. Write an opening line for a review of a new café.
  2. Replace "good" with a stronger adjective: The story was good from start to finish.
  3. Express a weakness politely: The service was slow.
  4. Write a recommendation for a film that is great for families.
  5. Which should take the most words in a review? (a) the plot (b) your opinion and reasons
Show answers
  1. For example: Looking for the perfect flat white? The new café on Corniche Road might be just what you need.
  2. The story was gripping from start to finish.
  3. The only drawback was the rather slow service.
  4. I'd highly recommend it to families with children of all ages.
  5. (b)
