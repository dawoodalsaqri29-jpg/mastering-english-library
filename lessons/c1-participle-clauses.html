Participle clauses

Combine ideas with -ing, past participle and having + past participle clauses for a tighter, more formal style.

Download PDF

A participle clause replaces a full clause with a participle. It makes writing shorter and more formal, and is common in reports, essays and news.

TypeFull clauseParticiple clause
Present participle (active, same time)As she walked home, she saw an accident.Walking home, she saw an accident.
Past participle (passive)The book, which was written in 1990, is still popular.Written in 1990, the book is still popular.
Perfect participle (earlier action)After he had finished the report, he left.Having finished the report, he left.
ReasonBecause I didn't know the way, I took a taxi.Not knowing the way, I took a taxi.
Reduced relative clauseStudents who live on campus pay less.Students living on campus pay less.
Negatives. Put not before the participle: Not wanting to be late, we left early.
Dangling participles. The subject of the participle must be the subject of the main clause. Walking into the room, the lights went off means the lights were walking. Say Walking into the room, I noticed the lights go off.

Practice

Rewrite using a participle clause.

  1. As I opened the door, I heard a noise.
  2. After she had read the email, she called her manager.
  3. The products, which are made in Oman, are sold worldwide.
  4. Because he didn't have a ticket, he couldn't enter.
  5. People who work from home save time.
  6. Correct the dangling participle: Arriving late, the meeting had already started.
Show answers

  2. Having read the email, she called her manager.
  3. Made in Oman, the products are sold worldwide.
  4. Not having a ticket, he couldn't enter.
  5. People working from home save time.
  6. Arriving late, I found that the meeting had already started.
