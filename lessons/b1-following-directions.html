Following directions

Understand spoken directions to a place: turns, landmarks and distances, in the right order.

Download PDF

When someone gives you directions, you need to remember a sequence: where to go first, where to turn, and what you will see. Listen for direction words and landmarks, and note them in order.

Listen forExamples
Movementgo straight on, go past, cross the road, go up the hill
Turnstake the second left, turn right at the lights
Landmarksopposite the bank, next to the pharmacy, on the corner
Distanceabout 200 metres, it's a five-minute walk

Listen

The recording is read by your device's built-in English voice. Listen first, then open the transcript to check.

Show transcript

Excuse me, how do I get to the city museum? Oh, it's not far. Go straight down this street until you reach the traffic lights. Turn left at the lights, and then take the second road on the right. That's King Street. Walk past the post office, and the museum is on your left, opposite a small park. It's about a ten-minute walk. If you get lost, just ask anyone near the park.

Draw while you listen. A quick arrow for each turn is faster than writing words, and easier to follow later.
Common mistake. Mixing up the second road on the right and the second right after the lights. Note where the counting starts.

Practice

  1. Where does the person want to go?
  2. What should they do at the traffic lights?
  3. Which road should they take after that?
  4. What building do they walk past?
  5. Where exactly is the museum?
  6. How long is the walk?
Show answers
  1. The city museum
  2. Turn left.
  3. The second road on the right (King Street)
  4. The post office
  5. On the left, opposite a small park
  6. About ten minutes
