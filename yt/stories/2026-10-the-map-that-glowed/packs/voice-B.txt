It was a map, its ink faded to a soft, rusty brown.

There was the village, a crooked oak, a winding stream and, on top of the highest hill, a tiny star.

“Whiskers, look,” Mia whispered. “Somebody drew our hills.”

Whiskers padded over, sniffed the paper, and stepped right onto the middle of it.

The moment his shimmering paw touched the parchment, a thin line of light began to glimmer across the faded ink.

It started at the village, curled past the crooked oak, and stopped, pulsing gently, beside the little star.

Mia was astonished. She rubbed her eyes, but the line was still there, glowing like a thread of sunshine.

“It’s showing us the way,” she breathed. “Whiskers, it wants us to follow it.”

Part of her felt reluctant. The sun was already low, and the hills could be lonely after dark.

But the glowing line seemed to tug at her heart, like a friend calling her name.

She climbed down the ladder, holding the map as if it were made of butterfly wings.

“Grandma, may Whiskers and I go for a walk on the hills?”

Grandma Nell glanced at the map, and her eyes twinkled. “Be home before the moon is high,” was all she said.
