Chapter five. The Treasure Under the Stars.

In the middle of the stones, half hidden by ivy, was a small round door set into the grassy hillside.

On it was carved a cat, sitting tall, its tail curled around its paws.

Mia pushed the door and pulled it, but there was no handle and no keyhole.

Then she remembered how the light had first appeared on the map.

“Whiskers,” she said softly, “would you touch the door, just like you touched the map?”

Whiskers stretched up on his back legs and placed one shimmering paw on the carved cat.

The carving flashed silver. With a deep, stony sigh, the little door swung open.

Mia ducked through the doorway, and gasped.

She was standing in a hidden hollow, open to the stars and filled with glowing flowers.

Hundreds of them, silver and blue, opened one by one in the moonlight, each one glimmering like a fallen star.

Mia was so astonished that, for a long moment, she could not speak at all.

In the centre of the hollow sat an old tin box, its lid painted with tiny faded stars.

Inside was a pouch of silver seeds, a child’s drawing, and a letter, folded small and just as brittle as the map.

The drawing showed a girl with a long plait and a black cat with bright green eyes.

“To the next brave child who follows the light,” the letter began. “I was nine when I found this place, with my cat, Shadow.”

“I thought the treasure would be gold. But the real treasure was the journey, and the friend who never left my side.”
