Chapter four. Lost in the Mist.

The path now climbed steeply towards the highest hill of all. They had never ventured so far from home.

As the sky turned violet, a white mist began to rise from the valleys below.

Soon Mia could barely see her own boots.

She took out the map, but in the thick grey mist its glimmer was faint and blurry, like a candle behind a curtain.

Somewhere nearby, the hillside dropped away into steep rocks. This was even more treacherous than the stream.

“Whiskers?” she called. Her voice sounded small and thin. “Whiskers, where are you?”

Then, from somewhere ahead, came a tiny, silvery sound. Ting.

It was the brass bell on Whiskers’ collar. Mia’s heart leapt. Of course!

“I can’t see in the dark,” she said slowly, “but you can. And I can’t see you, but I can hear you.”

Whiskers’ emerald eyes glowed through the mist like two small green lanterns.

Mia found a long yellow ribbon in her satchel and tied one end to his collar.

Holding the other end, she whispered, “Lead the way. I trust you.”

And so they climbed together, the cat in front and the girl behind, joined by a ribbon and a tiny ringing bell.

Whenever the edge came too close, Whiskers stopped and turned, guiding Mia safely away from the drop.

Ting, ting, ting. Step by step, they rose above the mist, and suddenly the night opened up around them.

A huge, pale moon was rising. Below them, the mist lay over the valleys like a silver sea.

And on the very top of the hill stood a ring of ancient standing stones.
