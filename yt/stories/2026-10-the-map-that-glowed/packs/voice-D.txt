Chapter three. The Treacherous Stream.

At the bottom of the valley, a stream rushed between the rocks, foaming white and silver.

The old bridge had broken long ago. Only two planks dangled over the water, creaking in the wind.

A line of stepping stones crossed the stream, but they were green with moss and slick with spray.

“This looks treacherous,” Mia said quietly. “One wrong step, and we’ll be swept away.”

Whiskers took one look at the water and sat down firmly. He was a cat, and cats do not like getting wet.

“I know you’re reluctant,” said Mia, kneeling beside him. “So am I. But the map says this way.”

She found a long stick and tested each stone before she trusted it.

The first stone wobbled. The second rolled. But the third stood firm and steady, like a little island.

One by one, Mia found the safe stones and remembered them with a little rhyme: “Flat, then round, then the big grey crown.”

She lifted Whiskers onto her shoulders, where he clung like a furry scarf.

Step. Splash. Step. The cold spray stung her cheeks, and the roar of the water filled her ears.

Halfway across, her boot slipped on the moss. She swayed, her arms whirling, and her knee struck the hard stone.

Her knee was scraped and stinging, and her eyes filled with tears. But she did not turn back.

Whiskers pressed his warm face to her cheek and purred, loud enough to hear above the water.

“All right,” Mia sniffed. “We’re resilient, you and I. We fall down, and we get back up.”

Then, one careful stone at a time, she finished the crossing.

On the far bank, she took out the map. The glowing line had grown brighter, as if it were proud of them.
