Chapter two. The Door Behind the Gears.

That night, Elias could not sleep. He sat by the stove, turning the key over and over.

Earlier, rushing to watch the snow, he had knocked his mother’s favourite blue jug off the table.

She had not shouted. She had only gathered the pieces in silence, and that was worse.

So, when the house was asleep, Elias slipped out into the night.

The square was silent and blue. Snowflakes drifted past the lanterns like slow, golden moths.

The tower door creaked open, and he climbed the winding stairs, round and round, into the dark.

At the top, huge brass gears turned in the shadows, and the great clock face glowed like a moon.

Something moved on a beam. Two round amber eyes blinked down at him.

It was a small grey owl, speckled with white, and he looked at Elias with a very solemn face.

“You must be the boy with Tobin’s key,” said the owl. “I’m Pip. I’ve been waiting for you.”

Elias nearly dropped his candle. “You can talk?”

“Only up here,” said Pip. “Time behaves strangely at the top of a tower.”

The owl hopped along the beam and tapped the wall. Behind the gears was a tiny keyhole.

Elias turned the key. With a sigh of warm air, a little round door swung open.

Inside was a secret room. Gold stars were painted on a midnight-blue ceiling, and a silver hourglass stood on a stone pedestal.

“Turn the hourglass,” said Pip, “and the whole town goes back one hour. But only once each night.”

Elias took a breath, put both hands on the cool silver, and turned it over.

At once, the great gears groaned and spun backwards. Outside, the snow floated up into the sky.

The candle flame shrank, then grew. And the bell rang out an hour that had already passed.

Elias raced home. There, on the kitchen table, stood his mother’s blue jug, whole and shining.

This time he walked slowly, and the jug stayed where it was.

His mother smiled at him over her sewing, and she never knew.
