Chapter three. Across the Misty River.

Soon they heard water, slow and heavy, somewhere in the dark.

The Misty River lay across their path, wrapped in a mist so thick that Noor could barely see her own hands.

Somewhere beneath that mist, she knew, were the old stepping stones. But where?

One wrong step, and the cold current would carry her away. It was the most perilous crossing in the whole forest.

Noor's knees began to tremble. “I can't do it,” she whispered. “I can't see a thing.”

Ember pressed his warm nose against her hand. Then, without a sound, he leapt into the mist.

He landed on a hidden stone, light and nimble as a falling leaf, and turned to look back at her.

And there, glowing faintly in the moonlight, was the white tip of his tail, bright as a little star.

“Of course!” Noor breathed. “I don't have to see the stones. I only have to follow you.”

Stone by stone, the fox led the way, and stone by stone, the girl followed the small white light of his tail.

Halfway across, Noor saw something tiny struggling on a wet rock: a firefly, its wings soaked and heavy.

Its light was dwindling to almost nothing, like the last breath of a candle.

Noor cupped it carefully in her palm and blew on it softly until its wings were dry.

The firefly shivered. Then it glowed, just once, warm and golden, before it flew away into the mist.

“Goodbye, little friend,” Noor smiled. Then, with one last jump, she reached the far bank beside Ember.
