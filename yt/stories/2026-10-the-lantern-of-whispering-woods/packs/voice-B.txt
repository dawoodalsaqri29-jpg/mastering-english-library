Just then, a sharp little cry came from the garden.

Noor pulled on her cloak and hurried out into the cold blue dark.

There, caught in the brambles by the gate, was a fox cub no bigger than a cat, with a coat the colour of autumn leaves.

“Hush now,” Noor murmured, kneeling down. “I won't hurt you.”

Slowly, gently, she untangled each thorn from his fur, even though the brambles scratched her fingers.

Once free, the cub didn't run away. He sat in the frosty grass and looked up at her with bright amber eyes.

“I'll call you Ember,” she smiled, “because you look like the last spark of a fire.”

Ember tilted his head. Then he trotted three steps towards the dark trees, stopped, and looked back at her.

“You want me to follow you?” Noor whispered. Her heart was beating fast, but her feet were already moving.

And so, under a thin silver moon, the girl and the fox stepped into the Whispering Woods.
