Chapter four. The Dark Hollow.

Beyond the river, the ground sloped down into a deep, shadowy hollow where no moonlight could reach.

Here the trees did not whisper at all. The silence was so eerie that Noor could hear her own heartbeat.

Black roots twisted overhead like the ribs of some enormous, sleeping creature.

At the bottom, the path split into three tunnels, each as black as the inside of a closed eye.

“Which way?” Noor whispered. Ember's ears flattened. For the first time, the brave little fox looked afraid.

Noor knelt and gathered him into her cloak. She could feel him trembling against her.

“It's all right,” she said softly, though her own voice shook. “We'll be steadfast together. We won't give up.”

Then she remembered an old trick of her grandmother's.

She licked one finger and held it up in the darkness, very still, and waited.

From the left tunnel came nothing. From the right came nothing. But from the middle came the faintest breath of cool, sweet air.

“Air means an opening,” Noor said. “This way leads out!”

The middle tunnel was darker than any night she had known, and the roots seemed to reach for her cloak.

Then, far ahead, a tiny golden light blinked.

It was the firefly from the river. And behind it came another, and another, and then a hundred more.

They poured through the tunnel like a river of stars, lighting every root and stone with a soft, radiant glow.

“You came back for us!” Noor laughed, tears shining in her eyes. Ember gave a happy yip and bounded ahead.

Guided by the fireflies, they climbed up and out of the hollow, into a circle of moonlight.
