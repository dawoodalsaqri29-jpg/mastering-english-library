Chapter five. The Lantern's Secret.

They stood at the edge of a round glade, ringed by ancient standing stones covered in silver moss.

In the very centre, on a pedestal of old grey stone, stood the Lantern.

It was taller than Ember and made of dark iron twisted like vines, with glass as clear as frozen water.

But it was empty. No wick, no oil, not even a trace of ash.

Noor's heart sank. She struck her flint again and again, but the sparks simply died against the glass.

“The Oak was right,” she said quietly. “No flint or flame can kindle it.”

The woods grew darker. Even the fireflies' light began to dwindle, and the cold crept closer.

Ember was shivering, exhausted. So Noor sat down on the cold stone, wrapped her cloak around him, and held him close.

“Whatever happens,” she whispered into his soft ear, “I'm so glad you came with me. I could never have done this alone.”

Ember looked up and gently licked her scratched fingers, the ones the brambles had torn when she set him free.

And at that very moment, a tiny golden spark appeared inside the Lantern.

Then another spark rose, drifting up from Noor's hands like warm pollen, and another floated from Ember's fur.

The thorns she had pulled from his fur. The firefly she had saved. The courage they had shared in the dark.
