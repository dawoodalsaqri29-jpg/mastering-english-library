Far away, the Elder Oak's deep voice rolled through the woods.

“The Lantern was never lit by fire, child,” it said. “It is lit by kindness given freely, and by courage shared.”

“For a hundred years, no one came with a kind heart, and so its light slowly faded.”

“But kindness is the only light that never truly goes out.”

The Lantern burst into light. Not the red of fire, but a warm, radiant gold, like sunrise poured into glass.

The light rolled out across the glade in a great golden wave, and the whole forest woke.

Mushrooms bloomed blue and violet beneath the roots. Thousands of fireflies rose from the grass like sparks from a joyful fire.

And the trees began to sing again, a deep and happy murmur that seemed to fill the whole sky.

Noor and Ember walked home through a forest that glittered like a jewel box.

The Elder Oak bowed its branches as they passed, and the river below them shone like liquid moonlight.

From that night on, the Whispering Woods never went dark again.

Noor grew up brave and kind, and wherever she went, a small red fox walked steadfast at her side.

And whenever anyone asked how she had lit the Lantern, Noor would smile and say,

“It's simple. Give a little of your light away, and somehow, there is always more.”
