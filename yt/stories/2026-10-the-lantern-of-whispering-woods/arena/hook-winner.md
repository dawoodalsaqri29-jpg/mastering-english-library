## 2. Why this one stops a scrolling viewer

- **The puzzle is in the first 3 seconds.** A Lantern that fire cannot light is a contradiction, and a contradiction is what makes a thumb stop. It is complete by 3.3 s, inside the 2-3 s window where the scroll is decided. It is spoken over the object itself, already moving with a push-in, because a calm voice cannot add energy and the picture has to.
- **The stakes come next, and you can see them.** In line 2 the forest's glow turns grey on screen as the narrator says it is going dark. The problem is clear by about 8 s: one object, it cannot be lit with fire, and without it the forest dies.
- **A new picture on every line.** The Lantern, the forest losing its light, the two heroes under bending trees, a black tunnel, then the Lantern again. Opening and closing on the same object makes the hook feel complete and makes the teaser loop.
- **It promises the journey and the ending.** Lines 3 and 4, over C5 and the Dark Hollow, promise a dangerous trip (chapters 2-4) without showing anything that happens on it. "What will light the Lantern?" is the question that only chapter 5 answers: kindness given freely and courage shared.
- **It gives nothing away.** It has no riddle, no river crossing, no fireflies and no answer, and the lit Lantern never appears. The baseline walked through the Oak, the river and the hollow, so a viewer had seen the whole trailer before the story began.
- **It works with the thumbnail.** The thumbnail asks "WHO LIT THE LANTERN?" and the hook asks *what* will light it. That is on purpose: line 3 already says who goes (one girl and one small fox), so a "who" question would be answered on screen at once. Together the two questions say: you know who goes, but not how it is done. The ending answers both: Noor and Ember lit it, with kindness and courage.
- **The English is clear for B1 learners.**
  - Short sentences in the simple present and "will".
  - Every "it" has only one possible referent. In line 2 the only noun before "it" is the Lantern, and line 3 names "the Lantern" again instead of using a pronoun.
  - Every word is B1 or below except **"Lantern"**, the key word of the story and of the thumbnail. The hook teaches it: the word is heard three times, the object is on screen at the first and last time, and «فانوس» sits under it in the Arabic box. It always has a capital L, so learners read it as one named object.
  - Each Arabic line is natural MSA that follows its English line closely, so the two boxes match.
  - Each line fits the 9:16 subtitle box in at most two lines.
- **It is true to the story.** The Elder Oak says "no flint or flame can light the Lantern". Grandmother says "as long as it shines, the forest shines too". The Oak warns "the way is perilous". The girl and the fox do go in together.
- **It works on its own.** It is under 20 seconds, with no dead air, and the loop restarts the story.

## Assumptions

- "Best hook" means the one most likely to stop a scroll in the first 2-3 seconds and keep the viewer watching the whole story on all three platforms. Shorter beats longer, as long as it opens a question for the journey and one for the ending.
- "What will light the Lantern?" implies the Lantern can be lit. The thumbnail already says it was, so nothing new is spoiled.
- Only the unlit L-R8 is used, because the lit version would give the ending away. B4 is left out because its warm mood would break the tension. L-R7 is shown only as a dark place.
