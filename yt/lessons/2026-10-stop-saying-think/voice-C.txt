Let's go deeper. Ponder or ruminate?

Ponder is neutral and calm. Ruminate is often negative: you go round in circles and can't stop thinking.

Presume or speculate?

When you presume, you're fairly sure. When you speculate, you're openly guessing, and you might be wrong.

And contemplate or deliberate?

You usually contemplate alone, about your own future. People deliberate together, before an official decision, like a jury, a committee, or a family.

Now, level up a sentence. Before: "I think the company will grow, and I am thinking about joining it."

After: "I envisage the company growing, and I am contemplating joining it." Same meaning, but it sounds like C1.

One more. Before: "I think he is busy, because he didn't answer."

After: "I surmise that he is busy, since he didn't answer." You're guessing from a clue, so surmise is perfect here.

Word families help you write more. Contemplate, contemplation. Deliberate, deliberation. Speculate, speculation, speculative. Presume, presumably.

Here are three ready-made sentence starters. One: "It is widely speculated that..."

Two: "After much deliberation, we decided to..."

Three: "I can't envisage a future without..."

Mistake number 1. Don't say: "I am thinking to travel next month."

After contemplate, use the -ing form, not "to".

Say it correctly with me: "I am contemplating travelling next month."

Mistake number 2. Don't say: "We discussed about the plan."

Discuss never takes "about". It's "discuss the plan".

Say it correctly with me: "We discussed the plan, and we deliberated on it."

Mistake number 3. Don't say: "In my opinion, I think that he is right."

Don't use both. Choose one, or a more precise verb.

Say it correctly with me: "I presume that he is right."

Mistake number 4. Don't say: "I envisage becoming a doctor in future."

When you mean later in your life, say "in the future", with "the".

Say it correctly with me: "I envisage becoming a doctor in the future."
