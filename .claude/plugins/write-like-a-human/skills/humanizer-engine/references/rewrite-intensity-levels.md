# Rewrite Intensity Levels

Three levels, one draft. The intensity controls how much of the original sentence structure and paragraph order survives, not just how many words change. Picking the right level matters more than any single word choice, so use this file to decide, then to show the buyer what each level actually does to the same text.

## The three levels, defined

### Light touch

**What it does:** Fixes vocabulary and removes obvious AI tells (clichés, hedging, abstract noun phrases). Varies a handful of sentence lengths where the uniformity is worst. Leaves sentence order, paragraph order, and overall structure untouched.

**Use it when:** The draft is already close to the buyer's voice, or the structure is genuinely good and only the surface reads stiff. Also right for time-pressured buyers who need a fast pass on something low-stakes.

**What NOT to expect:** This will not fool a careful human reader on its own, and it will not survive a deep AI-detection scan that looks at sentence-level structure rather than just word choice. It is a polish pass, not a rebuild.

### Moderate (the default)

**What it does:** Rewrites sentence by sentence in the buyer's voice. Breaks up uniform rhythm by combining, splitting, or reordering sentences within a paragraph. Applies the short-long-short discipline throughout. Structure of the piece (which point comes first, second, third) stays the same, but how each point is built and paced changes substantially.

**Use it when:** This is the right default for almost every AI-generated first draft. It is thorough enough to change the fingerprint of the writing, not just its vocabulary, while still being fast to review because the argument still flows in the original order.

### Full rewrite

**What it does:** Restructures freely. Reorders paragraphs, changes which idea opens the piece, cuts sections that do not earn their place, and rebuilds the argument in the order a person who already understood the point would explain it, rather than the order a model assembled it. Still protects every fact, number, and claim, but the sentences carrying them may end up in a different paragraph entirely.

**Use it when:** The draft is heavily AI-patterned (obvious three-part lists, a stock "in conclusion" close, every paragraph the same shape), when a lighter pass has already been tried and still reads generic, or when the piece is going somewhere that will get heavy scrutiny (a pitch, a feature launch, a piece under a byline that matters).

## Why intensity has to include structure, not just words

Synonym-swapping (replacing "utilize" with "use," "leverage" with "use," and so on) changes perplexity a little but leaves the sentence-length pattern and paragraph shape untouched. That pattern, not the vocabulary, is what a deeper read (human or automated) actually keys on. This is also why "Deep Scan"-style checks specifically target rewrites that look reworded but are not restructured. Every intensity level in this file, including light touch, includes at least some rhythm variation, because a rewrite that only swaps words is not humanization. It is a thesaurus pass.

## Worked example: the same paragraph at all three levels

**Original (AI-generated first draft):**

> In today's competitive marketplace, it is crucial for small businesses to leverage digital tools in order to enhance their customer engagement strategies. Furthermore, the implementation of a robust email marketing system can significantly improve conversion rates. It is important to note that businesses which fail to adapt to these evolving digital trends risk falling behind their competitors. Ultimately, embracing a comprehensive digital strategy is essential for sustained growth and long-term success.

That is four sentences, all in the 20 to 28 word range, all built the same way: a hedge, a claim, a stock transition, a repeat of the claim. No variation in length, no concrete detail, four abstract nouns doing the work ("engagement strategies," "implementation," "digital trends," "digital strategy").

**Light touch:**

> Small businesses need digital tools to keep customers engaged, especially now that the market is this competitive. A solid email system can noticeably lift conversion rates. Businesses that skip these tools risk falling behind. Building a real digital strategy matters for growth that lasts.

What changed: cliches and hedges gone ("crucial," "leverage," "furthermore," "it is important to note," "ultimately," "comprehensive," "essential"), abstract phrases replaced with concrete ones ("the implementation of a robust email marketing system" becomes "a solid email system"). What stayed: four sentences, same order, still fairly similar lengths (13 to 17 words). This is cleaner but still a little uniform. That is expected at light touch.

**Moderate:**

> Most small businesses are still guessing at customer engagement. That is a problem, because the ones who set up even a basic email system are seeing real lifts in conversions, not hypothetical ones. Skip that step and you are not just behind. You are invisible to the customers who already want to hear from you.

What changed: sentence count and order shifted (four sentences became four, but built differently: a short claim, a longer sentence carrying the evidence, a short consequence, a longer punch to close). Rhythm now runs short (7 words) to long (24 words) to short (7 words) to medium-long (18 words), which passes the short-long-short check. The abstract "comprehensive digital strategy is essential for sustained growth" is gone entirely, replaced by a sharper, more specific consequence. The core claim (email systems improve engagement and conversion) survives intact.

**Full rewrite:**

> Here is what most "digital strategy" advice skips: customers do not care that you have a strategy. They care whether you show up in their inbox with something worth opening.
>
> Set up an email system that actually does that and conversions move. Skip it, and you are not competing. You are already out of the running before the customer even knows you exist.

What changed: two paragraphs instead of one, a new opening line that leads with the actual insight instead of a throat-clearing setup, the "digital tools/trends/strategy" abstraction removed almost entirely in favor of one concrete mechanism (email showing up in an inbox). The claim about conversions survives. The order of ideas is now insight, mechanism, consequence, which is how a person who already believed this would actually argue it, not the setup-claim-hedge-conclusion shape the original used.

## How to choose in practice

Ask: is the structure of this draft already something a sharp human would have chosen, or is it the shape a model defaults to (three supporting points, hedge, "in conclusion")? If the structure works, light or moderate is enough and full rewrite would be wasted effort. If the structure itself is the tell, nothing short of full rewrite will fix it, because rhythm and vocabulary fixes on a bad skeleton just produce a better-dressed version of the same skeleton.
