# Before/After Gallery

Six short rewrites across six formats, all at moderate intensity (the default), so you can see the same principles applied to different content types. Each pair shows the AI-sounding original, the humanized version, and a one-line note on the main move.

## 1. Email (client check-in)

**Before:**

> I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out to provide you with an update regarding the status of the project. We have made significant progress on the deliverables and are on track to meet the agreed-upon deadline. Please do not hesitate to reach out if you have any questions or concerns.

**After:**

> Quick update on the project. We're on track for the deadline, and the deliverables are further along than I expected at this point. Anything you want me to walk you through before then, just say the word.

**Main move:** Cut the throat-clearing opener and the boilerplate closer entirely. "I hope this email finds you well" and "please do not hesitate to reach out" carry zero information; a person who actually knows the recipient does not write them. Also replaced a passive, vague progress claim with something that reads like an actual opinion ("further along than I expected").

## 2. LinkedIn post (thought leadership opener)

**Before:**

> In today's rapidly evolving business landscape, leadership is no longer just about making decisions, it's about empowering your team to navigate uncertainty with confidence. As organizations continue to embrace digital transformation, the most successful leaders are those who cultivate a culture of continuous learning and adaptability.

**After:**

> Most leadership advice is about making better decisions. That's not the hard part anymore. The hard part is getting your team to move when nobody's sure what the right decision even is.

**Main move:** Full structural swap, not a reword. The original opens with a "not just X, it's Y" cliche and a generic claim about "the business landscape." The rewrite leads with a contrarian, specific point and earns the reader's attention in one shorter sentence before expanding. Also cut "empowering," "navigate," "embrace," "cultivate," and "landscape" entirely rather than softening them.

## 3. Blog intro (how-to article)

**Before:**

> In this comprehensive guide, we will delve into the essential strategies for optimizing your website's search engine performance. Whether you're a seasoned marketer or just starting out, this article will provide you with valuable insights to help you navigate the complex world of SEO.

**After:**

> Most SEO advice is written for people who already understand SEO. This is not that. Here's what actually moves your rankings, in the order that matters, whether you've done this before or you're starting from zero.

**Main move:** Removed the two most reliable AI tells in the sentence ("comprehensive guide," "delve into") and the meaningless hedge to both audiences ("seasoned marketer or just starting out ... valuable insights"). Replaced with a specific promise (order that matters) instead of a vague one (valuable insights).

## 4. Product description (ecommerce)

**Before:**

> Our innovative water bottle is designed to enhance your hydration experience. Featuring a state-of-the-art insulation system, this bottle keeps beverages cold for extended periods, making it the perfect companion for your active lifestyle. Elevate your daily routine with this must-have accessory.

**After:**

> This bottle keeps drinks cold for 24 hours, even left in a hot car. No sweating through your bag, no lukewarm water halfway through a hike. Fill it once in the morning and forget about it.

**Main move:** Replaced every abstract claim with a concrete, checkable one. "Enhance your hydration experience" and "elevate your daily routine" say nothing measurable; "keeps drinks cold for 24 hours, even left in a hot car" is a claim a reader can picture and test. Cut "innovative," "state-of-the-art," "perfect companion," "must-have," and "elevate" without replacing them with synonyms, because the sentence is stronger with nothing in their place.

## 5. Proposal paragraph (freelance scope)

**Before:**

> Moving forward, our team will leverage a robust, data-driven approach to ensure the successful implementation of your marketing strategy. We are committed to fostering a collaborative partnership and delivering measurable results that align with your business objectives.

**After:**

> Here's how this works. We build the campaign around your numbers, not a template, and you'll see weekly reporting so you know exactly what's working before we scale it. If something's not pulling its weight, we cut it. No quarter-end surprises.

**Main move:** Full rewrite of a paragraph that was pure abstraction (leverage, robust, data-driven, foster, collaborative partnership, measurable results, business objectives, none of it specific to this client). Replaced with concrete operating details (weekly reporting, cutting underperformers) that a buyer can actually evaluate and hold the freelancer to.

## 6. Landing page section (SaaS feature)

**Before:**

> Our platform empowers teams to seamlessly collaborate and streamline their workflows, resulting in increased productivity and efficiency. With our comprehensive suite of tools, you can unlock your team's full potential and drive meaningful results across your organization.

**After:**

> Your team is probably losing an hour a day to tools that don't talk to each other. This one does. Everything lives in one place, so nobody's hunting through five tabs to find the file someone else already finished.

**Main move:** Opened with a specific, relatable problem instead of a list of benefit words (empowers, seamlessly, streamline, comprehensive, unlock, meaningful) that describe every SaaS product equally and therefore describe none of them. The rewrite is checkable (a reader either does or does not recognize the tab-hunting problem) where the original was not.

## What all six have in common

Every rewrite here did at least one of these, usually two or three:
- Cut a stock AI phrase entirely instead of finding a fancier synonym for it.
- Replaced an abstract claim with something specific enough to be picture-able or checkable.
- Changed sentence order or count, not just word choice, within the paragraph.
- Shortened the opening. AI drafts almost always over-explain before making the point; humans tend to make the point first.

None of them changed a number, a name, a date, or the actual claim being made. That is the boundary this skill holds on every pass, at every intensity.
