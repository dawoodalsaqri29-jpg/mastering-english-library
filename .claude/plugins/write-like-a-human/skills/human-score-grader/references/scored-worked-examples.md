# Scored Worked Examples

Three full before/after pairs, each scored across all 8 dimensions of `8-dimension-rubric.md`, with reasoning shown per dimension. These demonstrate the real point jump a genuine humanizing pass produces, and what "discriminating" scoring actually looks like in practice: bad drafts score badly across most dimensions, not clustered in the safe middle.

---

## Example 1: LinkedIn post from a B2B software founder

**Original (unedited AI draft):**

"In today's fast-paced business landscape, effective communication is more crucial than ever. Organizations that leverage robust communication strategies often see a comprehensive improvement in team alignment and overall productivity. Furthermore, it's important to note that seamless collaboration tools can help unlock significant value for teams navigating complex projects. At [Company], we are committed to empowering our clients with innovative solutions that drive meaningful results. In conclusion, prioritizing communication is a crucial step toward organizational success."

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 3/15 | Every sentence lands in the 18 to 24 word medium-long band. No fragment, no short punch, no real long sentence either, just five sentences of nearly identical shape in a row. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 2/15 | Almost every noun and verb is the safest available choice: "effective communication," "robust communication strategies," "meaningful results." Nothing is concrete to this company or this founder. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 2/15 | Textbook template: throat-clearing open ("in today's fast-paced business landscape"), generic middle claims, summary conclusion that literally starts with "in conclusion." |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 1/15 | No voice profile signal anywhere; this could be posted by any B2B company in any industry with zero changes beyond the bracketed name. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 0/15 | Nine watch-list hits in roughly 85 words: "in today's fast-paced," "crucial" (twice), "leverage," "robust," "comprehensive," "furthermore," "it's important to note," "seamless," "unlock," "empowering," "innovative," "in conclusion." Density this high is an automatic zero. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 6/10 | Not heavily hedged in the reflexive-qualifier sense, though "can help unlock" softens a claim that did not need softening. Most of the loss here is really a cliche problem bleeding into this dimension. |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 0/10 | Zero specific people, moments, clients, or numbers anywhere in the piece. Entirely abstract. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 0/5 | Reads as unmistakably AI-generated from the first six words. |
| **Total** | **14/100** | Band: 0-19, unedited AI output. |

**Rewrite (after humanizer-engine at full intensity, matching a founder whose profile lists tone words "blunt," "impatient with fluff," "story-first"):**

"Most teams don't have a communication problem. They have a 'nobody knows who owns this' problem. I watched a 12-person team lose three weeks last quarter because two people both assumed the other was handling client follow-up. Nobody was. The client noticed before we did. That's not a tooling gap. That's a habit gap: nobody had a five-minute weekly check where blockers actually get said out loud instead of buried in a Slack thread from Tuesday. We built [Product] around that one habit. It's not fancy. It just makes the check happen whether people remember to or not."

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 14/15 | Wide swing: "Nobody was." (2 words) next to "I watched a 12-person team lose three weeks last quarter because two people both assumed the other was handling client follow-up." (22 words). No run of same-band sentences anywhere. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 13/15 | Concrete throughout: "12-person team," "three weeks," "a Slack thread from Tuesday." "Habit gap" versus "tooling gap" is a specific, non-generic distinction, not a safe default phrase. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 13/15 | Opens on a reframe, not a topic restatement. Leads with the anecdote before the claim, which is backward from template order and reads more like real thinking. Ends on what the product actually does rather than a summary. |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 14/15 | "Blunt" and "story-first" both show up directly: the opening line is a blunt reframe, the whole middle is a specific story. "It's not fancy" reads like an impatient-with-fluff aside. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 15/15 | Zero watch-list hits. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 10/10 | No reflexive hedges; every claim is stated directly, including the flat "That's not a tooling gap. That's a habit gap." |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 10/10 | Fully specific: a 12-person team, three weeks, a named failure mode, a client who noticed first. Doing real persuasive work, not bolted on. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 5/5 | Reads exactly like a founder telling a real story, no hitch. |
| **Total** | **94/100** | Band: 80-100, genuinely human. |

**Delta: +80 points.** Biggest jumps: cliche density (0 to 15), structural predictability (2 to 13), and narrative presence (0 to 10). This is the pattern a full-intensity restructuring pass should produce: not just cleaner words, a different shape entirely.

## Example 2: Client email from a wedding photographer

**Original (AI-assisted first draft, light edits only):**

"I hope this email finds you well. I wanted to reach out regarding your upcoming wedding on the 14th. It's important to note that our team is fully committed to ensuring your special day is nothing short of perfect. We utilize a comprehensive approach to capturing every meaningful moment, and our goal is to deliver a seamless experience from start to finish. Please don't hesitate to reach out if you have any questions or concerns. We look forward to being part of your journey."

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 5/15 | Five sentences, all in the 12 to 20 word band. Some minor variance but nothing under 10 words or over 22, no real swing. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 4/15 | "Comprehensive approach," "meaningful moment," "special day," all the safest phrase available in each slot. Nothing names what this photographer actually does differently. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 6/15 | Standard business-email template (opening pleasantry, reassurance, closing offer to help) but at least it is short enough that the template does less damage than a longer piece would show. |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 2/15 | No trace of an individual photographer's voice; reads like a template any studio could send. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 3/15 | Four watch-list hits in about 80 words: "it's important to note," "utilize," "comprehensive," "seamless," plus "journey" used in the closing line, five total. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 7/10 | Not heavily hedged, reasonably direct for what it is, though "nothing short of perfect" is filler-confidence rather than a real claim. |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 4/10 | No specific example, though the piece is short and transactional enough that a full anecdote is not strictly required; scored relative to what a short logistics email needs, not penalized to zero. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 1/5 | Reads like a template, though not egregiously robotic; a busy person might not flag it, a careful reader would. |
| **Total** | **32/100** | Band: 20-39, clearly stiff or templated. |

**Rewrite (after humanizer-engine at moderate intensity, matching a photographer whose profile lists tone words "warm," "direct," "a little playful" and a quirk of always naming a specific detail about the couple):**

"Hi Sam and Jordan, three weeks out from the 14th. Here's where things stand on my end: I'll arrive at the venue by 1pm to get ahead of the light before the ceremony, and I already have your shot list flagged for the reception, especially that first-dance moment you mentioned during our call. If anything about the timeline shifts between now and then, just text me, I'd rather hear about it early than find out at 2pm on the day. Otherwise, I'm set. Can't wait for this one."

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 13/15 | "Otherwise, I'm set." and "Can't wait for this one." are short punches after a long, detail-carrying middle sentence. Real swing across the piece. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 13/15 | "Get ahead of the light," "2pm on the day," "that first-dance moment you mentioned" are all specific to this actual job, not swappable into a generic template. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 12/15 | Skips the opening pleasantry entirely and opens on logistics, which breaks template. Closes on genuine warmth rather than a generic offer to help, though the middle is still a fairly standard status-update shape. |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 14/15 | "Warm," "direct," and "a little playful" all present; the signature quirk of naming a specific couple detail (the first-dance moment) shows up directly. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 15/15 | Zero watch-list hits. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 9/10 | Direct throughout; "I'd rather hear about it early" is a clear preference stated plainly, not hedged. |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 8/10 | Grounds the email in specific, real logistics (1pm arrival, the first-dance moment from an actual call) rather than staying abstract; not a full story but appropriately concrete for a short logistics email. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 5/5 | Reads exactly like a specific photographer emailing two specific clients. |
| **Total** | **89/100** | Band: 80-100, genuinely human. |

**Delta: +57 points.** Biggest jumps: voice match (2 to 14) and cliche density (3 to 15). Note that this rewrite did not need a huge structural overhaul (structural predictability only moved 6 to 12) because the original's problem was mostly vocabulary and lack of voice, not shape. This is a useful contrast with Example 1: not every low score needs the same fix.

## Example 3: Blog intro paragraph for a management consulting firm

**Original (unedited AI draft):**

"Organizational change management is a multifaceted discipline that requires a holistic understanding of both people and process. Many companies struggle to navigate the complexities of digital transformation, often due to a lack of comprehensive planning. It is crucial for leadership teams to embark on this journey with a clear strategy and a commitment to innovative thinking. Ultimately, successful transformation requires robust change management practices that empower employees at every level of the organization."

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 4/15 | Four sentences, 15 to 24 words each, all medium-long, no real swing anywhere in the paragraph. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 1/15 | Nearly every phrase is a stock consulting phrase: "multifaceted discipline," "holistic understanding," "navigate the complexities," "clear strategy." Nothing is specific to any real client, industry, or situation. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 3/15 | Classic consulting-blog template: broad claim, problem statement, prescriptive advice, summary claim. Could open any article on this topic with zero rewriting. |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 1/15 | No individual firm's voice anywhere; interchangeable with any consulting firm's blog. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 0/15 | Eight watch-list hits in roughly 75 words: "multifaceted," "holistic," "navigate," "comprehensive," "crucial," "embark," "journey," "innovative," "robust," "empower," (ten, actually, several doubled up in one sentence). Automatic zero. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 5/10 | Not classically hedged with "might" or "could," but "many companies struggle" and "often due to" are vague-scope claims doing the same evasive work as a hedge. |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 0/10 | Zero specific companies, situations, or moments. Entirely abstract, and this is a blog intro that could easily support one concrete example. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 0/5 | Reads as generated corporate boilerplate from the first sentence. |
| **Total** | **14/100** | Band: 0-19, unedited AI output. |

**Rewrite (after humanizer-engine at full intensity, matching a consultant whose profile lists tone words "sharp," "a little dry," "leads with the uncomfortable truth" and vocabulary notes favoring short declarative sentences):**

"Most transformation projects fail for a boring reason: nobody told the people doing the actual work why it mattered to them. We worked with a 400-person manufacturing client last year who had a beautiful new process map and a workforce that quietly kept doing things the old way for six more months. The map wasn't the problem. The silence was. Change management isn't a discipline you bolt onto a project plan at the end. It's the answer to one question, asked early and often: what's actually in this for the person changing?"

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence rhythm variance (0-15) | 14/15 | "The map wasn't the problem. The silence was." are two short, punchy fragments back to back for emphasis, right after a 30-word scene-setting sentence. Strong, deliberate swing. |
| Vocabulary predictability (0-15) | 14/15 | "A beautiful new process map," "quietly kept doing things the old way," "bolt onto a project plan" are all specific, slightly unexpected word choices, not the safest available option. |
| Structural predictability (0-15) | 13/15 | Opens on a blunt claim, not a topic restatement. Leads with a specific client story before any general principle. Ends on a question instead of a summary, which is the opposite of template order. |
| Voice match to profile (0-15) | 14/15 | "Sharp," "dry," and "leads with the uncomfortable truth" are all directly present: "for a boring reason" and "the map wasn't the problem, the silence was" are exactly that move. |
| Cliche and stock-phrase density (0-15) | 15/15 | Zero watch-list hits. |
| Hedging density (0-10) | 10/10 | Fully direct; "the map wasn't the problem, the silence was" is about as unhedged as a claim can be. |
| Narrative and anecdote presence (0-10) | 9/10 | A specific client (400-person manufacturing company), a specific timeframe (six more months), and a specific concrete detail (the process map) all doing real work. Not a full named case study, but strongly grounded. |
| Overall natural read (0-5) | 5/5 | Reads like a consultant who has actually seen this happen, not a document describing the concept of it happening. |
| **Total** | **94/100** | Band: 80-100, genuinely human. |

**Delta: +80 points.** Same pattern as Example 1: the dimensions that moved most are cliche density, structural predictability, and narrative presence, which are exactly the dimensions a synonym-only pass cannot fix. Vocabulary and structure both needed genuine restructuring, not word swaps, matching the research finding that detection accuracy drops sharply once text is actually restructured rather than lightly reworded.

## What these three examples show about scoring discipline

- Bad AI drafts do not score in the 40s. They score in the teens because they fail almost every dimension simultaneously, not just one.
- The dimensions that jump the most after a full-intensity rewrite are almost always cliche density, structural predictability, and narrative presence, since those require actual restructuring rather than vocabulary polish.
- A moderate-intensity rewrite (Example 2) can still reach the 80s if the original's core problem was voice and vocabulary rather than structure. Not every low score needs a full teardown; match the fix to what is actually broken.
- Voice match only scores high when the rewrite uses something specific from the buyer's actual profile (a named tone word, a real quirk), not just "sounds professional and warm."
