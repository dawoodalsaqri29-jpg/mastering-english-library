# Specificity Upgrade Examples

The swipe table tells you which words to cut. This file teaches the underlying method: how to take a vague, jargon-heavy sentence and rebuild it around something specific, checkable, and real. Eleven worked examples across common contexts, each with the method named so it transfers to sentences not covered here.

## The core method

Every fix below does one of these three moves:

1. **Replace an abstract noun phrase with the concrete action or thing it stands in for.** ("The implementation of the new system" becomes "we rolled out the new system" or, better, what the new system actually does.)
2. **Replace a vague claim with a number, name, or mechanism.** ("Significantly improved performance" becomes "cut load time from 4 seconds to 1.2.")
3. **Replace a category word with a specific member of that category.** ("A variety of tools" becomes naming the actual two or three tools.)

Find which move applies before rewriting. Guessing at a fix without identifying the move usually produces a fancier vague sentence instead of a specific one.

## Worked examples

### 1. Abstract noun phrase, business update

**Before:** "The implementation of the new onboarding flow resulted in an improvement in user retention."

**Method:** Move 1 (concrete action) plus move 2 (real number).

**After:** "We rolled out the new onboarding flow in March, and 30-day retention went from 41% to 58%."

### 2. Vague benefit claim, product marketing

**Before:** "Our platform enhances team productivity and streamlines collaboration."

**Method:** Move 3 (name the specific mechanism, not the category of benefit).

**After:** "Your team stops emailing spreadsheets back and forth. Everyone edits the same file, and you can see who changed what and when."

### 3. Hedge plus abstraction, client email

**Before:** "It's worth noting that there has been a notable increase in engagement across various channels."

**Method:** Cut the hedge (Category 2 of the swipe table), then move 2 (real numbers) and move 3 (name the channels).

**After:** "Instagram comments doubled and email open rate went from 22% to 31%, both since the new format launched."

### 4. Hype word with no backing, About page

**Before:** "We are a comprehensive, innovative solution for all your marketing needs."

**Method:** Move 3 (name the actual services instead of the category "all your needs") and drop the unearned hype words entirely rather than replacing them.

**After:** "We handle your email campaigns, your paid ads, and your landing pages. If it's not one of those three, we'll tell you who to call instead."

### 5. Corporate jargon stack, internal memo

**Before:** "We need to leverage our core competencies to drive synergies across departments."

**Method:** Move 1 (concrete actions) for every jargon word in the sentence, since this one has three stacked together.

**After:** "Design and engineering need to actually talk before a launch, not just after. That's the fix, not a new process document."

### 6. Vague claim of scale, case study

**Before:** "Our solution has helped numerous businesses achieve significant growth."

**Method:** Move 2 (real number) and move 3 (name at least one specific business or scenario, even anonymized).

**After:** "Fourteen of our clients hit their first $10,000 month within 90 days of starting. One of them had tried three other approaches first and none of them worked."

### 7. Empty intensifier, product description

**Before:** "This tool is incredibly powerful and remarkably easy to use."

**Method:** Move 2 (specific proof of both claims) since "incredibly" and "remarkably" are unearned intensifiers with nothing behind them.

**After:** "You can build a working automation in under five minutes, no code, and it handles the kind of workflow that used to take a developer half a day."

### 8. Metaphor overload, mission statement

**Before:** "We're on a journey to unlock the full potential of every entrepreneur, navigating the ever-changing landscape of small business."

**Method:** Cut every metaphor (journey, unlock, navigate, landscape), then move 1 to state plainly what the company does.

**After:** "We help entrepreneurs get their first ten paying customers without burning their savings doing it."

### 9. Vague process description, proposal

**Before:** "Our team will utilize a data-driven approach to optimize your marketing strategy and drive measurable results."

**Method:** Move 1 (name the actual approach) and move 2 (define what "measurable" actually means here).

**After:** "We'll run your campaign against your actual sales numbers, not just clicks, and send you a report every Friday showing what's working and what we're cutting."

### 10. Overused contrast structure, LinkedIn post

**Before:** "This isn't just a productivity tool, it's a mindset shift."

**Method:** The "not just X, it's Y" structure (Category 5 of the swipe table) usually hides a claim that was never made specific. Cut the structure and state what actually changes.

**After:** "Most people use this for fifteen minutes and then stop opening five other apps to find what they need."

### 11. Buzzword-dense feature list, SaaS landing page

**Before:** "Our robust, seamless integration ecosystem empowers you to unlock actionable insights across your entire tech stack."

**Method:** This sentence has five flagged words in one line (robust, seamless, ecosystem, empowers, unlock, actionable insights, six really). Cut every one, then move 3 to name the actual integrations and what the "insight" actually is.

**After:** "Connect it to your CRM and your ad accounts, and it tells you which leads are actually worth calling first, based on what they did before they filled out the form."

## How to self-check a fix

After rewriting, ask: could this sentence, word for word, describe a different company or a different product with zero changes? If yes, it is still too generic, no matter how many buzzwords you already removed. A genuinely specific sentence should only fit the thing it is actually describing.
