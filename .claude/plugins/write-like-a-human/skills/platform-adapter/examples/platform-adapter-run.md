# Platform Adapter: Worked Example

Sample brand (for illustration only): Renata Voss, a solo attorney who blogs occasionally and wants the same core update adapted for her email list and for LinkedIn, where she has a modest but engaged following of other solo practitioners.

## Input draft (pasted by the buyer, already humanized, platform-agnostic)

"This month I made the decision to stop offering hourly billing entirely and move to flat-fee packages for all new clients. This was a significant change to my business model. The transition required careful planning and consideration of various factors, including client expectations and revenue predictability. So far, the results have been positive, and I believe this approach will benefit both my practice and my clients going forward."

Requested platforms: email (to her client and prospect list) and LinkedIn (to her peer network).

## Step 1: Identify the core idea

Renata switched from hourly to flat-fee billing this month, it was a real decision with some risk, and early results are good. That's the whole idea; everything else in the source draft is generic filler around it.

## Step 2 to 3: Load specs, rebuild hooks

Email spec: conversational, direct address, one clear ask. LinkedIn spec: text-only, natural line breaks, hook must survive truncation, no formal sign-off.

Renata confirms one added detail when asked: she made the call after realizing she'd been undercharging a client by nearly half for a flat-fee-shaped project she kept billing hourly.

## Steps 4 to 6: Reshape, sign off, trying-too-hard check

**Email version delivered:**

Subject line: A change to how I bill, starting this month

"Quick update, since a few of you have asked how billing works with me going forward.

As of this month, I've moved to flat-fee packages for all new client work, no more hourly billing. The short version of why: I did the math on a project last year and realized I'd undercharged by almost half because the hourly clock didn't account for how efficient I'd actually gotten at that kind of work. Flat-fee fixes that, for both of us. You know the number going in, and I'm not penalized for getting faster at my job.

If you're a current client, this doesn't change anything on work already in progress. If you're bringing me something new, I'll send a flat-fee quote up front instead of an hourly estimate.

Questions about how this works for your situation, just reply and ask.

[NAME]"

**LinkedIn version delivered:**

"I did the math on a project from last year and realized I'd undercharged myself by almost half.

Not because my rate was wrong. Because I'd gotten faster at the work, and hourly billing punishes exactly that. The better I got, the less I made.

So this month I moved every new client to flat-fee packages. No more hourly billing.

It's a real change, and it took me longer to make than it should have, mostly because 'this is just how legal billing works' is a hard assumption to question when everyone around you is doing it the same way.

One month in: clients know their number up front, I'm not punished for efficiency, and I'm not tracking six-minute increments anymore. If you're still billing hourly and it's never quite sat right, that feeling is worth listening to."

**Change log shown to Renata:** Both versions rebuilt around the undercharging detail (buyer-supplied, not in the original draft, flagged for her confirmation per her fact-guard setting). Email version added a direct ask (reply with questions) and a note on current-client continuity, neither of which existed in the source. LinkedIn version cut the ask entirely per platform convention and ended on a direct statement instead. Neither version reused the source draft's phrase "significant change to my business model," since it read as filler in both target platforms.
