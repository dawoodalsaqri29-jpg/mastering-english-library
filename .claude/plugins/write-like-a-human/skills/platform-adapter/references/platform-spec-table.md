# Platform Spec Table

The maintained source of truth for how each platform's native conventions work. Load this before adapting to any platform rather than relying on general knowledge, since these norms shift and this table is what keeps every skill run current.

## Email

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| Ideal length | 150 to 300 words for a nurture or relationship email; up to 500 for a detailed update. Shorter wins more often than longer. |
| Formatting norms | Short paragraphs, 1 to 3 sentences each. Plain text or near-plain text over heavy HTML design. One clear ask, stated directly, not buried in paragraph three. Subject line under 50 characters, specific rather than clever. |
| Hook convention | Conversational, direct address, opens like a message to one person, not a broadcast to a list. Often opens mid-thought, referencing something specific ("Quick one on the thing you asked about last week") rather than a formal greeting-plus-preamble. |
| Sign-off convention | A natural, personal sign-off that matches how the writer actually signs things (first name only, a short closing line, sometimes a P.S. that carries a secondary point or a lighter aside). Never a corporate block signature in a relationship email. |
| "Trying too hard" tells | Multiple asks crammed into one email. A subject line with urgency bait ("Don't miss this!!"). Overly formal opening ("I hope this email finds you well"). A wall of unbroken text. Signing off with a full title and company block when the relationship is personal. |

## LinkedIn

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| Ideal length | 200 to 400 words. Long enough to earn a real point, short enough to read in one sitting without a "see more" fatigue drop-off. |
| Formatting norms | Natural line breaks (one to two sentences per visual line), not artificial one-word-per-line formatting. Text-only reads as more human and gets more genuine replies. Heavy Unicode bold, excess checkmarks, and emoji bullet stacks now read as generated and get penalized by readers and the platform alike; use formatting sparingly, if at all. |
| Hook convention | The first one to two lines must work standalone, since LinkedIn truncates with "see more." A specific claim, a story cold-open, or a direct address to a named audience works better than a vague teaser. |
| Sign-off convention | No formal closing. The post ends on the point, sometimes with a short question to invite comment, but not a "Thoughts?" tacked on reflexively. No signature block. |
| "Trying too hard" tells | Unicode bold text throughout. A checkmark or emoji at the start of every line. Excessive line breaks that turn three sentences into fifteen lines. A hook that promises a story and delivers a listicle. Hashtag stacks of five or more at the bottom. Humble-brag framing ("I don't usually share this, but..."). |

## X (Twitter)

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| Ideal length | Single post: under 280 characters ideally, tight and punchy. Thread: 5 to 12 posts, each one able to stand alone. |
| Formatting norms | Minimal formatting. No heavy hashtag use (0 to 1 max, usually zero). Line breaks used for rhythm and emphasis, not decoration. |
| Hook convention | Compression is the whole game. First-person, raw over polished, a claim or scene that stops the scroll in one line. No throat-clearing, no setup before the point. |
| Sign-off convention | None. The post or thread ends on the sharpest line, not a summary or a call to "let me know your thoughts." A thread's last post can include one direct, low-key call to action (a link, a follow prompt) but never a hard sales close. |
| "Trying too hard" tells | Hashtag stacking. Overly polished, press-release phrasing. A thread that pads to hit a round number of posts. Engagement-bait closers ("Retweet if you agree"). Corporate voice softened into blandness; X rewards a sharper, more first-person edge than other platforms. |

## Blog

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| Ideal length | 800 to 1,500 words for a standard post; up to 2,500 for a deep guide. Length should match the depth of the idea, not a target word count. |
| Formatting norms | Scannable structure (subheadings, occasional short lists) without collapsing into listicle-itis, where every paragraph becomes a bullet and the piece loses its narrative thread. Mix prose paragraphs with structure; don't fragment everything. |
| Hook convention | A narrative or expertise-driven opening: a scene, a specific claim, or a real question the writer is answering from experience. Never a dictionary-style definition opener ("X is defined as..."), which is one of the clearest AI tells in long-form writing. |
| Sign-off convention | A close that either resolves the opening story/question or gives a clear, singular next step (not three competing CTAs). No generic "In conclusion" framing. |
| "Trying too hard" tells | A definition-style opener. Excessive subheadings that break up a single idea into six shallow sections. Keyword-stuffed phrasing clearly written for search engines over readers. A conclusion that just restates the introduction. Transition clichés ("In today's fast-paced world," "It's important to note that"). |

## YouTube script

| Dimension | Spec |
|---|---|
| Ideal length | Matches target video runtime: roughly 130 to 150 spoken words per minute. A 5-minute script lands around 650 to 750 words. |
| Formatting norms | Written for the ear, not the eye. Contractions throughout. Sentence fragments are fine and often better than a full sentence. Short lines. Stage directions or on-screen text cues can sit in brackets alongside the spoken line. |
| Hook convention | Spoken-cadence cold open in the first 5 to 10 seconds, direct address to the viewer, often a question, a bold claim, or dropping straight into the middle of a moment. No "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel" preamble before the hook. |
| Sign-off convention | A direct-to-camera call to action, spoken plainly (subscribe, watch the next video, comment a specific answer), not a written-style closing paragraph. |
| "Trying too hard" tells | Sentences that are grammatically correct but unspeakable out loud (too long, too many clauses). Written-register words a person would never actually say ("henceforth," "moreover," "utilize"). A hook that takes 20 seconds of preamble to get to the point. Reading like an essay with "um" inserted rather than actually being written for speech. |

## Quick-reference length bands

| Platform | Ideal length |
|---|---|
| Email | 150 to 300 words (up to 500 for detailed updates) |
| LinkedIn | 200 to 400 words |
| X (single post) | Under 280 characters |
| X (thread) | 5 to 12 posts |
| Blog | 800 to 1,500 words (up to 2,500 for deep guides) |
| YouTube script | ~130 to 150 words per minute of runtime |
