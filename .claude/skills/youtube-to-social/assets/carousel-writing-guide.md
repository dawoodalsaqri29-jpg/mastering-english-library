# Carousel Writing Guide

Principles, length conventions, and shape patterns for writing strong carousels. Read this every time the skill produces a carousel.

Carousels don't use a format menu the way scripts do. The substance dictates the structure — the skill's job is to walk a reader through it across 5-7 slides with one clear idea per slide. This file is the reference for *how* to write each slide, not a checklist of formats to pick between.

---

## Length and structure conventions

**Slide count.** 5-7 slides is the sweet spot. Cover (slide 1) + 3-5 content slides + close (last slide). Use the substance's density as the guide — never pad to a fixed count, never compress a strong idea into half a slide just to hit a number.

**Per-slide text length.** Portrait or 4:5 carousels: under ~35 words per slide. Square (1:1): under ~25 words. Stories (9:16): under ~20 words. Whitespace is what makes a carousel look premium. Dense text-walls look amateur.

**Cover slide.** This is the only slide read at full attention — it has to earn the swipe. The cover is a hook: a question, a claim, a number, a contrast, a curiosity gap. **No setup. No preamble. No "in this carousel I'll cover..."** Just the hook.

**Content slides.** One idea per slide. If a slide has two ideas, split it. Each slide should stand alone — if someone screenshots only that slide, they should still get something from it. This is what drives saves.

**Last slide.** Earns the action. If the buyer has a CTA configured, include it. Otherwise close on a one-line takeaway that ties the whole carousel together — the kind of line readers will save or screenshot.

---

## Cross-cutting principles

**Specificity is the multiplier.** "A budget meal" → "a five-star dinner for $4.83." "A common mistake" → "Don't say 'just checking in' in a follow-up email." Pull specific numbers, names, dates, and examples from the transcript onto the slides. Vague substance produces vague carousels.

**One idea per slide.** Carousels reward narrowness. If you find yourself trying to fit two ideas on one slide, split it.

**Each slide stands alone.** Don't write slide 4 such that it depends on slide 3 to make sense. Write each slide so that a screenshot of it on its own would still land — that's what makes carousels savable and shareable.

**The cover earns the swipe.** Treat slide 1 like a thumbnail — its only job is to make the reader tap to slide 2. Hooks that work: a contrarian claim, a surprising number, a single bold question, a contrast that doesn't add up.

**The last slide earns the action.** If you ask the reader to do something — save, share, follow, click — say so explicitly on the last slide. "Save this for later" or "Follow for more" are fine if the substance was strong.

**No reference to the source video.** No "I watched this video", "in this video", "the speaker said", or any similar credit. The substance transfers (facts, frameworks, numbers) but the voice and framing are the buyer's.

## Common shapes (apply organically — these are NOT a menu)

When you look at the substance after Step 3 of the workflow, the shape of the carousel will usually be obvious. These are the patterns that tend to emerge — pick whichever fits, or combine, without asking the buyer to choose between them.

### Tier walk

When the substance has clear escalating tiers or levels (price tiers, skill levels, capability tiers, sophistication tiers).

- **Cover:** Name the count and the spectrum. "5 levels of [thing] — beginner to master." or "What [thing] looks like at $50, $200, and $2,000."
- **Content slides:** One tier per slide. Headline = the tier label. Body = what defines this tier and why it matters.
- **Close:** Where most readers actually are + what to do about it.

### List

When the substance has a small number of distinct, parallel items (5 things, 7 mistakes, 4 reasons).

- **Cover:** Promise the count and the topic. "5 [things] that [outcome]." Specific count + specific outcome.
- **Content slides:** One numbered item per slide. Headline = the item in 3-7 words. Body = 1-2 sentences of elaboration.
- **Close:** The strongest item restated as a takeaway, or the CTA.

Don't pad to a number. A 4-item list with strong items beats a 7-item list with two weak ones.

### Story arc

When the substance has a setup → development → reveal shape, especially with an unexpected ending.

- **Cover:** A line that sets up the wrong reading or the unresolved question. "I thought [X]. Then [single fact]." or "What everyone gets wrong about [Y]."
- **Content slides:** Walk the story across slides — establish the framing, add a piece of evidence, add another, drop the reveal.
- **Close:** Recontextualize the whole story in light of the reveal.

### Claim + receipts

When the substance makes a strong, doubtable claim and has concrete evidence to back it up.

- **Cover:** State the claim in one bold line. "[Specific contrarian claim]."
- **Content slides:** One piece of evidence per slide — specific numbers, dates, examples, screenshots described in text.
- **Close:** What the evidence collectively proves + CTA.

### Then vs. Now

When the substance is a clear before-after transformation with verifiable states.

- **Cover:** Side-by-side or stark contrast. "[Past state] vs. [present state]."
- **Content slides:** Specific decisions or moments that bridged the gap. One shift per slide.
- **Close:** What the transformation proves + CTA.

### Question + answer

When the substance answers a specific yes/no or how question that the audience would naturally wonder about.

- **Cover:** The question itself. Specific, closed-ended.
- **Content slides:** Build evidence in both directions — what suggests yes, what suggests no.
- **Close:** The answer, then what it means in practice.

---

## Stacking shapes

Carousels often combine two shapes: a "tier walk" inside a "claim + receipts" frame, a "list" with a "then vs. now" intro, etc. Don't stack just to stack — stack only when each shape earns its keep. Default to a single shape that fits cleanly.

---

## When the substance doesn't suggest a shape

Default to a hook + content + close skeleton:

- **Cover:** A specific question, claim, or contrast pulled directly from the substance.
- **Content slides:** Each slide develops one beat of the answer or explanation.
- **Close:** A one-line takeaway + the CTA if set.

This always works. Reach for it when no specific shape above fits cleanly.
